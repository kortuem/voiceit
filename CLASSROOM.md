# Using VoiceIt in a class or workshop

VoiceIt was developed for IDEM307 — Generative AI and Design at TU Delft. This guide keeps the classroom framing separate from the [general tutorial](TUTORIAL.md). It is not a current assignment brief, assessment rule or requirement for independent users.

## A group workflow

Work in one laptop's `voiceit` folder, with one agent session if you use an AI assistant. Take turns directing the work and demonstrate from that laptop. A GitHub account is not needed for local work. Keep observations in `design/notes.md`; they can start a plenary discussion.

The design sequence is: plan a scene on paper, write or ask an agent to write its script, inspect the script, play it, judge it and revise. The plan belongs to the designers. An assistant should read it back before writing and ask before adding events or changing their order.

If comparing two products, keep the people and events in the scene the same while changing the products' responses. Compare what they say, whom they address, what they show instead of speaking, and when they stay silent. Pair the behaviours with different forms and discuss how the impression changes.

## The hospital examples

The original material uses a patient and others around a bedside product, with contrasting product characters. Those are worked examples, not a required setting. Teachers can choose another context; an independent designer can simply use their own project.

The example scenes are scripted enactments. They do not call a real nurse, operate a device, understand speech or supply medical advice. Evaluate the proposed interaction, not whether a real service was performed.

## Optional exercise: contrasting stereotypes

The original guidance below is retained from `system/BOUNDARIES.md` at release 0.5.1. It describes an optional exercise, not the default brief for every project.

Groups sometimes contrast products through stereotypes: a very masculine and a very feminine product, for example. These limits apply to that, and to everything else you make with VoiceIt.

- **Express stereotypes through manner and behaviour**: initiative, amount, register, whom the product addresses, what it takes on, how it handles doubt, its voice and its form. That is enough to make them recognisable.
- **No insults, slurs or demeaning jokes** about any group, and no sexual content or innuendo, in lines, rules, images or form.
- **Write the people in the script with respect**, even when the product does not treat them so.
- **A group may refuse** to build a stereotype. Design a contrast of your own instead, and be ready to say why.

For the agent: if a request crosses these limits, say so plainly, point to this file, and offer a version that shows the stereotype through behaviour instead. Do not lecture; continue with the work.

## Before a session

- Try the supplied Timer example on the actual laptops and speakers. Voice quality and folder access differ by browser and operating system; the [README](README.md#voices-and-timing) records testing limits.
- Agree how participants will save and share their own files. VoiceIt is not a submission service or shared project store.
- Use fictional or cleared material. Opening a local folder does not upload it to a VoiceIt service, but browser speech voices may use an online service; AI authoring tools have their own data handling.
- Check the current course brief separately for any deliverables. This repository does not impose a deadline, a required number of products or an assessment scheme.
