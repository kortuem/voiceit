# Situations

A situation is what happens around the device: who is there, what they want, what goes wrong. It says nothing about what the device does. That is conduct, and it differs per device.

## The file

`design/situations/<name>.md`, with a short name in lowercase and hyphens (`visiting-hour`):

```
---
title: Visiting hour on ward 4
people: ANNA, DAAN, LOTTE, DE WIT, OKAFOR
---
1. 15:00. Anna Visser, 74, recovering from pneumonia, sits up in a two-bed room. Mrs de Wit in the other bed is trying to rest.
2. Her son Daan and her granddaughter Lotte, 8, arrive. Lotte is curious about the device.
3. …
```

- `title`: a name people will recognise.
- `people`: everyone who may speak, in capitals, separated by commas. These are the names used in the screenplay. Anyone else speaking is flagged by the check.
- Then the **beats**: a numbered list, one line each, usually four to seven.

## Writing beats

- One beat is one thing that happens or one thing someone wants.
- Write what happens, not what the device does. "Daan asks about the test results" is a beat; "The device explains the test results" is conduct.
- Give each beat something at stake: a need, a conflict, a person who should not hear something, someone who is asleep.
- Put the time and place in the first beat.
- Several people in the room make conduct visible: the device has to decide whom it addresses and what it says aloud.

Every performance of this situation keeps all the beats, in order. The device may make a beat go differently, but it may not skip one.
