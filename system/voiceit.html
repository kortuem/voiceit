What this is

VoiceIt is a tool for prototyping things that listen and respond: a lamp, a bedside unit, a car, any object that could hear people and speak. A thing's behaviour is hard to judge on paper. It has to be seen and heard, in time, among people. VoiceIt stages it before the thing exists.

You work with two kinds of material. A form is what the thing looks like: one image. A behaviour is how it acts in one situation: a script with speech, screen, light and sound. Any behaviour can be played with any form.

The character is not written down anywhere. It is what comes across when a form and a behaviour are played together, and it is what you judge.

How to work

  1. Behaviour. Tell your agent, in your own words, who the thing is and how it should behave in a situation. It writes the script into design/behaviours/. Play it, and give notes: less, later, softer, not in front of her.
  2. Form. Sketch the thing on paper, have it rendered, mark up the render and render again (system/FORM.md). Save the image in design/forms/.
  3. Judge. Play behaviours with different forms, and compare them side by side.

What to judge

  • Fit. Does this behaviour suit this form? Try it in another one.
  • Coherence. Is it the same character from one situation to the next?
  • The details that make a character. When does it speak first, and when does it wait? How much does it say? Whom does it address when several people are there? What does it keep to itself, or show instead of saying? How does it handle doubt and mistakes? Is it a servant, a companion, a guardian, a colleague?

What it cannot do

Everything is scripted. Nothing listens; listening is shown, not done. People's reactions are written, so they will not surprise you the way real people do. Voices come from your browser and only approximate the voice named in the script. VoiceIt is for exploring many versions quickly, before building one that works.

Open a folder

Background

VoiceIt belongs to a family of methods for trying out behaviour before a system exists: Wizard-of-Oz studies, experience prototyping and enactment. It prototypes a thing's role and its look and feel over time, not its implementation, and it aims at many rough variations rather than one refined prototype.

A thing's character, in design research, is a coherent quality that holds across its appearance and its behaviour. Among several people, a device also has to decide whom it addresses, who merely overhears, and when to leave people alone.
